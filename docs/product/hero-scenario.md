## 1. Decision and purpose

This document freezes one coherent end-to-end story for product decisions, wireframes, architecture, test fixtures, demonstrations, and release acceptance. It prevents different teams from demonstrating different products under the StreetSherlock name.

The hero narrative is intentionally split into two release checkpoints:

1. **StreetPulse checkpoint — `v0.1.0-streetpulse-mvp`:** a public-space report becomes a human-reviewed incident decision and a safe citizen update.
2. **InfraProof checkpoint — `v0.5.0-infraproof`:** the same incident may later be connected to a previous repair, evidence, inspection, and a possible warranty recurrence.

The complete story is the product vision. Only the first checkpoint is part of the one-month StreetPulse MVP. The second checkpoint must not be simulated as if it already exists in the MVP.

## 2. Scenario statement

After heavy rainfall, several citizens report loose or sunken paving and standing water on the same cycle-path segment in a synthetic Deventer scenario. StreetSherlock protects sensitive report content, produces structured advisory information, shows possible related incidents with explainable evidence, and asks a municipal employee to decide whether the reports describe the same real-world problem. A deterministic policy recommends a priority, an authorized employee confirms or overrides it, the incident status changes, and each affected citizen receives a safe status update.

In the later InfraProof release, the incident can be compared with a previous utility repair at the same location. The system may identify a possible recurrence and image changes, but an inspector—not AI—decides whether evidence is sufficient and whether any repair or warranty workflow should continue.

## 3. Non-negotiable scenario rules

- A `Report` is independent source evidence; an `Incident` is the human-owned representation of a real-world problem.
- No report is deleted, overwritten, or silently merged.
- Duplicate results are candidates, not decisions.
- AI may structure text, identify limitations, and generate advisory assessments; it cannot make an official operational decision.
- Priority is calculated from a versioned deterministic policy, not generated by an LLM.
- An authorized employee confirms or overrides priority and records the reason required by policy.
- Restricted originals, contact data, safe derived data, and public output remain separate.
- Failure of AI, privacy processing, weather data, n8n, or notification delivery must not lose the report or invent a decision.
- Street works, repair evidence, computer vision, recurrence, warranty handling, contractor communication, and PDF evidence packages are outside the StreetPulse MVP.
- A possible recurrence is not proof of causation, defective work, contractual liability, or contractor fault.
- All people, reports, addresses, coordinates, identifiers, images, weather observations, organizations, and outcomes used in the scenario are synthetic or approved fixtures.
- The demo must not state or imply that the Municipality of Deventer is a customer, partner, data provider, or reviewer.

## 4. Actors and authority

| Actor/component | Scenario responsibility | Authority boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Citizen/reporter | Submits Dutch or English text, a map location, optional contact data, and optional media; later views a public-safe update. | Does not create an official incident, select final priority, or see internal/restricted information. |
| Intake employee | Reviews privacy/assessment outcomes and possible report-to-incident links. | Accepts or rejects a candidate; the system cannot do this on the employee's behalf. |
| Case handler | Reviews the priority recommendation, performs an allowed incident transition, and approves public-safe status information. | Owns official operational decisions within assigned permissions. |
| Privacy processor | Validates content, separates storage zones, removes approved metadata, and creates safe derived content. | May block publication or request review; does not discard the source report. |
| AI text provider | Returns schema-validated advisory facts, missing information, and limitations. | Cannot invoke tools, query business data directly, change state, or trigger a workflow. |
| Candidate engine | Retrieves and scores possible related incidents using bounded, explainable factors. | Cannot create a report-to-incident link. |
| Priority engine | Calculates a recommendation from a versioned deterministic demo policy. | Cannot set the final operational priority. |
| StreetSherlock backend | Enforces authorization/state rules, persists business state, audit evidence, and notification intent. | PostgreSQL remains the business source of truth. |
| n8n/notification adapter | Delivers an approved notification intent and reports delivery status idempotently. | Cannot directly change incident, decision, or notification-intent authority. |
| Inspector — later release | Reviews comparable evidence and possible recurrence. | Owns inspection and evidence-sufficiency decisions. |
| Contractor user — later/pilot | Receives only an authorized assignment or approved notice. | Never sees reporter contact data and cannot decide municipal liability or acceptance. |

## 6. StreetPulse MVP flow

| Step | Trigger and processing | Advisory output | Required human action | Persistent/audit evidence |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A citizen enters report text, selects a map point, optionally adds contact data/media, reviews the submission, and submits. | Client/server validation messages. | Citizen corrects invalid input or confirms submission. | Source, event time, consent/notice version where applicable, correlation ID, and immutable source reference. |
| 2 | The backend validates file type/signature/size/dimensions, separates contact data and restricted originals, strips approved metadata from derived output, and runs privacy processing. | Safe derived text/media or `needs_privacy_review`. | A permitted employee corrects or approves output when automatic processing is insufficient. | Restricted object reference, derived object reference, transformation version/status, limitations, and reviewer. |
| 3 | The AI provider processes only the permitted representation and returns strict structured JSON. Java validates category, enums, coordinates, evidence, and schema again. | Summary, language, category, danger/obstruction facts, missing information, and limitations. | Employee corrects/uses the assessment or continues manually. | Provider/model/prompt/schema versions, latency, outcome/error class, original output, and human correction. |
| 4 | Deterministic municipality/status/time/category/spatial filters retrieve possible incidents; optional semantic evidence contributes only after bounded retrieval. | Ranked candidates with every factor, missing signal, threshold, freshness, and plain-language explanation. | Intake employee reviews each relevant candidate. | Candidate set/version, factor values, inputs, thresholds, and retrieval time. |
| 5 | The employee accepts `INC-2001` and rejects `INC-2002`. | A proposed link command and validation result. | Authorized employee confirms the link/rejection. | Attributable `ReportIncidentLink`, rejection reason where required, source reports preserved, and reversible history. |
| 6 | The deterministic priority engine evaluates verified safety, obstruction, route, weather, and available context under `demo-priority-policy/0.1`. | Explainable `P2 high` recommendation plus missing/stale factors and policy version. | Case handler confirms or overrides with a recorded reason. | Recommendation and final decision stored separately with actor, time, policy, evidence, and reason. |
| 7 | The case handler performs the allowed `OPEN` → `VERIFIED` transition for `INC-2001` and approves a public-safe status message. | Valid/invalid transition result and public preview. | Authorized case handler confirms the transition and public message. | Previous/new state, actor, reason/context, version, optimistic-lock result, and safe timeline event. |
| 8 | The backend stores a notification intent and outbox event in the same transaction. The adapter sends the approved update through the local demo channel and reports status. | Delivery attempt/status. | No new product decision; an operator may retry a failed delivery. | Intent ID, idempotency key, template/version, attempts, error class, callback, and final delivery state. |
| 9 | The citizen opens the tracking view. Staff open the internal incident timeline. | Public-safe status for the citizen; full authorized decision/provenance view for staff. | None beyond role-appropriate access. | Authorization result and complete trace from reports to incident decision and delivery. |

### StreetPulse checkpoint outcome

The demo passes the MVP checkpoint when all three source reports still exist, the accepted and rejected candidate decisions are attributable, the final priority is human-owned, `INC-2001` is in the authorized target state, the citizen update is delivered at most once, and restricted data is absent from public output.

## 7. Safe failure and recovery paths

| Failure | Required safe behaviour | Recovery/evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Invalid or suspicious upload | Reject the file before processing; preserve valid form fields where safe; show an accessible reason. | Citizen removes/replaces the file; rejection reason and correlation ID are logged without storing unsafe content unnecessarily. |
| Privacy processing fails or confidence is below policy | Keep the restricted original inaccessible to public/contractor roles and block publication. | Route to `needs_privacy_review`; a permitted reviewer corrects/approves a safe derivative. |
| AI times out, refuses, returns malformed JSON, or is unavailable | Store no invented assessment and do not block report preservation. | Route to manual review with provider/error metadata; employee enters/corrects structured facts. |
| No candidate is returned | Do not force a link. | Authorized employee creates/continues a separate incident path; decision is audited. |
| Strong but incorrect candidate is returned | Score remains advisory; no automatic link. | Employee rejects it, records a reason where required, and the outcome becomes evaluation evidence. |
| Candidate evidence is missing/stale | Show the missing/stale factor; do not silently score it as negative or current. | Renormalize according to documented policy or require review; record the version used. |
| Priority input is missing or stale | Show an incomplete recommendation or require review; never fabricate a final class. | Employee verifies inputs and confirms/overrides under an authorized policy path. |
| Unauthorized user attempts a decision or sees a restricted original | Deny by default and change no business state. | Record a privacy-safe security/audit event and require an authorized role. |
| Invalid or conflicting state change | Reject the transition or optimistic-lock conflict. | Refresh current state, re-evaluate, and resubmit an explicit command. |
| n8n or mail transport is unavailable | Preserve committed incident state and the pending notification intent. | Retry with the same idempotency key; show delivery state to staff. |
| Callback/retry is duplicated | Treat it as the same delivery attempt and do not send a second citizen message. | Record duplicate/idempotent handling and retain one authoritative result. |
| Weather/source fixture is unavailable | Show unavailable/stale context and do not treat absence as safe conditions. | Use a dated approved fixture or proceed without the factor under documented rules. |

## 8. Later InfraProof continuation — outside `v0.1.0`

After the StreetPulse checkpoint is stable, `v0.5.0-infraproof` may continue HS-01:

1. A synthetic earlier utility street work, work polygon, contractor record, completion, accepted repair, evidence, and configurable warranty window are available.
2. Street memory shows spatial/temporal overlap between `INC-2001` and the repair footprint.
3. The system creates a `possible_recurrence` candidate, never an automatic warranty claim.
4. A field inspector uses guided capture and quality checks to obtain a comparable current image.
5. InfraProof validates/decode inputs, may align images, highlights possible change regions, and refuses unsuitable comparisons.
6. The inspector confirms, corrects, or rejects findings and selects an authorized outcome such as `accept`, `monitor`, or `rework_required`.
7. Any draft report is produced only from approved structured facts, remains marked `draft`, and requires inspector approval.
8. The backend may create an approved evidence package and notification intent; n8n transports it but cannot change authoritative state.

InfraProof is successful only when evidence limitations and human ownership are visible. It must never claim that image similarity or location overlap proves causation, liability, warranty validity, or contractor fault.

## 9. Given/When/Then acceptance walkthrough

### HS-AT-01 — Preserve and process a valid report

**Given** `RPT-1001` contains valid Dutch text, a synthetic map point, optional contact data, and supported media  
**When** the citizen reviews and submits it  
**Then** the report is persisted independently, restricted/contact/derived/public zones are separated, the transformation lineage is recorded, and the citizen receives a tracking reference.

### HS-AT-02 — Block unsafe publication

**Given** the restricted image for `RPT-1001` contains a synthetic face or licence-plate test object and privacy processing is incomplete  
**When** a public or contractor view requests the media  
**Then** the original is not returned, publication remains blocked, and a permitted reviewer sees a privacy-review task.

### HS-AT-03 — Validate advisory AI output

**Given** the permitted report representation is sent through the configured AI provider  
**When** valid structured output is returned  
**Then** Java validates it again, the assessment is labelled advisory, versions/limitations are stored, and no incident or priority state changes.

### HS-AT-04 — Degrade safely without AI

**Given** the AI provider times out, refuses, is unavailable, or returns malformed output  
**When** report processing continues  
**Then** the source report remains safe and usable, no facts are invented, and the employee can complete the structured review manually.

### HS-AT-05 — Require human duplicate review

**Given** `INC-2001` and `INC-2002` are returned with visible factor explanations  
**When** the intake employee accepts `INC-2001` and rejects `INC-2002`  
**Then** only the accepted link is created, all reports remain independent, both decisions are attributable, and the link history is auditable/reversible.

### HS-AT-06 — Require a human-owned priority

**Given** `demo-priority-policy/0.1` produces an explainable `P2 high` recommendation  
**When** the case handler reviews it  
**Then** the handler must confirm or override it, any required reason is stored, and recommendation and decision remain separate records.

### HS-AT-07 — Enforce authorization and incident transitions

**Given** `INC-2001` is `OPEN`  
**When** an authorized case handler changes it to `VERIFIED`  
**Then** the backend validates the transition and records the actor and old/new state; the same request from an unauthorized actor changes nothing.

### HS-AT-08 — Deliver one safe citizen update

**Given** the authorized incident change commits with a public-safe notification intent  
**When** the workflow is retried or the callback is duplicated  
**Then** the citizen receives at most one message and staff can see the delivery attempt/status without n8n changing incident authority.

### HS-AT-09 — Prove complete traceability

**Given** the StreetPulse checkpoint is complete  
**When** an authorized reviewer opens the internal timeline  
**Then** the report sources, privacy actions, assessment versions, candidate factors, human decisions, priority policy, transition, notification attempts, and correlation IDs are traceable in order.

### HS-AT-10 — Keep release boundaries honest

**Given** the running version is `v0.1.0-streetpulse-mvp`  
**When** the hero demo reaches the citizen update  
**Then** the demo stops and labels street works, repair lineage, guided capture, computer vision, inspection, recurrence, warranty, contractor workflow, and evidence PDF as later InfraProof scope.

## 10. Repeatable demo script

1. Show the synthetic-data banner and identify the StreetPulse checkpoint.
2. Submit `RPT-1001`; show safe validation and separate data zones without exposing restricted content.
3. Show the advisory structured assessment and its model/schema/limitation labels.
4. Open the intake queue; compare `INC-2001` with the negative `INC-2002` candidate.
5. Accept one candidate and reject the other; show that every source report remains present.
6. Review the deterministic priority explanation; confirm or override it as the case handler.
7. Perform the authorized incident transition and preview the public-safe message.
8. Trigger delivery, simulate one duplicate retry/callback, and show one resulting citizen message.
9. Compare the public tracking timeline with the richer authorized audit timeline.
10. Stop the MVP demo. Show the InfraProof continuation as a labelled roadmap/wireframe only.

## 11. Open external questions

These do not change the frozen portfolio scenario, but they must be answered before a real pilot:

- Which municipal roles own candidate review, priority policy, status, notification approval, and service levels?
- Which lawful bases, retention/archive periods, publication rules, and data-subject procedures apply?
- Which MOR, zaak, BOR/asset, identity, map, work, document, and contractor systems are authoritative?
- Which priority factors/classes and weather thresholds are approved by a design-partner municipality?
- Which warranty clauses, evidence standards, appeal/rework paths, and contractor permissions apply?
- Which independent privacy, security, accessibility, AI-governance, and operational reviews are required?

No answer may be invented or presented as Deventer policy.
